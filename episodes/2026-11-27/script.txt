MIA: Okay, conditioning, because this is where I have scars. I tried persona prompts for a stakeholder survey and every "respondent" landed between sixty and seventy.
GEORGE: That's the known failure. Santurkar and colleagues documented it in twenty twenty-three: models collapse toward the modal opinion. Reinforcement learning from human feedback rewards the typical, agreeable answer, so personas regress to the middle. And Hansen and co-authors in twenty twenty-five simulated the Survey of Professional Forecasters with personas, but could only match distributions, because those responses are anonymised.
MIA: So what's the fix here?
GEORGE: Three blocks of information. First, a firm profile: industry, revenue band, headcount, share of foreign sales, headquarters, credit rating. Second, web search limited to before the cutoff date. Third, and this is the contribution, the respondent's own history. Up to twelve past scores from the last twelve quarters, their average, and the model's own earlier forecasts for that person. The system prompt tells it to treat the history as "a strong prior on this individual's style, optimism bias, and level."
MIA: So you give each persona its own past.
GEORGE: And that restores individual spread. Think of it as giving an impressionist a recording of the actual person rather than a description.
