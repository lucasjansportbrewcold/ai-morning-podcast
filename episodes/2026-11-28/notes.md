## Deep dive: Looped transformers. Thinking in depth instead of in tokens, and what it means for local agents

- Why this topic, why now:
  On 20 November 2026 Thinking Machines released Inkling 2 Mini, a 26.4B-parameter open-weights looped transformer (Apache 2.0) that runs on a 32 GB Mac. Earlier in November NVIDIA published Dory1.0-4B, the first open looped model in its Nemotron Labs family. That makes two serious, post-trained, agent-capable looped models you can download today. Looping adds a third way to spend compute, next to model size and chain-of-thought length: run the same layers several times per token, "in latent space". For Lucas this touches three things he cares about: (1) running agents locally on sensitive data at low cost, (2) the trade-off between accuracy, tokens and latency, and (3) monitorability: if the model thinks less in words, can you still watch what it reasons? It's a Saturday architecture topic that has not been a deep dive before. (Inkling 2 Small was a news item on 16 November; Mini and the looped architecture were not covered.)

- Outline:
  1. **The mechanism: reuse layers instead of adding them.** A normal transformer gets more capable by adding layers, each with its own weights, or by writing a longer chain of thought. A looped model stores a block of layers once and sends each token's hidden state through it several times. The structure is a "sandwich": an input block that runs once and turns tokens into internal representations, a shared recurrent block that runs k times, and an output block that runs once and turns the final state back into token predictions. In Inkling 2 Mini, 24 stored layers become 48 layer applications per token. In Dory, 72 physical layers become 32 + 40k applications, so 72 to 192. The extra thinking happens in the hidden state and never shows up as text. Lineage: Universal Transformers (Dehghani et al., 2019) through ByteDance's Ouro (2025) to these 2026 models.
  2. **Making the loop trainable and stable.** Naively looping a network is unstable: Ouro saw loss spikes at 8 recurrent steps and dropped back to 4. The two 2026 models use different tricks. Inkling 2 Mini: every pass restarts from the prelude output, and passes 2 and 3 also receive a "message" from the previous pass through a learned gate. That gate starts fully closed, so at first each loop behaves like a non-looped model, and it opens during training. Dory: the input representation is re-injected before every loop, with L2 normalisation on a normalised-transformer (nGPT) backbone. Training samples the loop count from {1,2,3,4} and backpropagates only through the last loop (truncated backprop, depth one). Training at mixed loop counts is what lets one checkpoint serve several compute budgets ("elastic depth").
  3. **Where to loop, and what looping buys.** Thinking Machines tested looping the middle layers against looping the last layers. The middle loop gave a 1.51–1.91× compute-equivalent gain, the last loop 1.24–1.36×. Their explanation: the first and last layers mostly translate between tokens and internal representations, and the middle layers carry the more abstract reasoning. On what it buys, the evidence agrees across all three sources: looping improves *manipulating* knowledge (multi-hop reasoning, coding, agentic tasks) but does not add *stored* knowledge. In Ouro, looped and non-looped models both store about 2 bits per parameter. In Dory and Inkling, knowledge-recall scores are flat across loop counts. Practical rule: looping can't make a small model know more facts, so pair it with retrieval.
  4. **Latent compute replaces token compute, so the cost maths changes.** More loops make each token more expensive but cut the number of tokens. In Inkling 2 Mini's research model, mean GPQA Diamond reasoning tokens fall from 3,703 at 1 pass to 1,731 at 3 passes. In Dory on agentic tasks, four loops need 1.3–2.5× fewer output tokens per attempt than one loop, mostly because the agent needs fewer turns (GDPval-AA v2: 116 to 49 turns). NVIDIA's approximate compute accounting says two loops cost only 1.03–1.25× the compute of one loop but recover most of the four-loop gain. Their recommended default is k=2, with k=4 when accuracy matters most. This is the same theme as the 16 November "effort is the new prompt" item: price per token doesn't predict cost per task.
  5. **The serving catch: memory, caches, quantisation, tooling.** Looping saves weight memory but not compute, and with standard caching each loop keeps its own KV cache, so cache memory grows linearly with k. Ouro found that sharing caches during prefill costs more than 10 points on GSM8K, but at decode time keeping only the last step's cache loses almost nothing and uses 4× less memory. Dory's "last-loop KV caching" keeps KV memory at the one-loop size for any k, changing scores by −2.12 to +0.42 points at k=4. Dory can also do self-speculative decoding: the same checkpoint drafts at k=1 and verifies at k=4, for a 1.11–1.53× speedup in request duration. Runtime support is young: the Inkling card says looped-model support in local runtimes is new and speed varies.
  6. **Monitorability: does reasoning you can't read become a problem?** Looped models think partly without words, which weakens chain-of-thought monitoring as a control. Thinking Machines measured this directly. The variable-loop model's no-CoT time horizon (task length, in human time, solved at 50% reliability without any chain of thought) rises from 16 seconds at one pass to 37 seconds at three, then flattens. In a LinuxArena control evaluation (a helpful-only attacker fine-tune, with a weaker Qwen3.6-35B-A3B as monitor), stealth rates showed "no clear trend with the number of looped passes". Their conclusion: looping "increases what these models can do without a verbalized chain of thought," but "at this scale we do not see a meaningful reduction in the monitorability." The open question is what happens at larger scale.

- Facts and examples with sources:
  - **Inkling 2 Mini (primary: Thinking Machines announcement and Hugging Face model card)**
    - Released 20 November 2026; open weights; Apache 2.0 license; text and image input, text output; context up to 1M tokens. [primary] (https://huggingface.co/thinkingmachines/Inkling-2-Mini)
    - MoE with 26.4B total parameters, 3.4B active, about 5.4B weight activations per token when the loop is counted. [primary] (https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/)
    - 24 layers in four blocks of six. Block 1 (prelude) and block 4 run once; blocks 2 and 3 run three times, giving 48 layer applications per token. Each token is routed to 8 of 128 experts per layer. Attention is a hybrid of local and global layers. [primary] (https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/; https://huggingface.co/thinkingmachines/Inkling-2-Mini)
    - Every pass starts from the prelude output; passes two and three also get a "message" from the previous pass, merged through a learned gate that starts fully closed. [primary] (https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/)
    - Expert pruning: training started with 8 of 384 experts active; midtraining pruned two-thirds, keeping the 128 most-used experts per layer. After pruning, the model was "healed" with 1T tokens distilled from a larger internal Inkling model. [primary] (https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/)
    - Training: 4-bit NVFP4 for expert layers in pretraining and RL, last four layers in MXFP8. RL totalled 2.2T tokens over 2,000 steps, plus on-policy distillation. [primary] (https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/)
    - A non-looped run needs over 1.5× the compute to match the looped model's held-out loss. Middle-layer looping: 1.51–1.91× compute-equivalent gain; last-layer looping: 1.24–1.36× (measured at training step 2,000). [primary] (https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/)
    - Research variant (TML-d24-varloop-research): trained with 1–5 passes, mostly at 3; RL only at 3 passes. No-CoT 50% time horizon: 16 s at 1 pass, 37 s at 3, roughly flat beyond. GPQA Diamond mean reasoning tokens: 3,703 (1 pass), 1,731 (3), 1,661 (5). Knowledge recall (AA-Omniscience, SimpleQA Verified, SimpleVQA, WorldVQA) nearly unchanged across pass counts. [primary] (https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/)
    - Monitorability: CoT controllability (can the model deliberately reshape its chain of thought, e.g. avoid a keyword?) at most 0.1% for Inkling 2 Mini and the 3-pass variant, against 1.3% for Qwen3.8-27B; the authors say the test can't separate loop counts at such low rates. LinuxArena stealth rate (tool-calls-only monitor, 1% false-positive threshold, 184 main/side-task pairings): 1.0% (1 pass), 2.5% (2), 4.0% (3), 2.8% (4), 5.0% (5). With text and thinking visible to the monitor: 0.0–0.6% for the variable-loop model, 1.3% for Inkling 2 Mini. [primary] (https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/)
    - Benchmarks (lab-reported): DeepSWE v1.1 48.2%, Vals Index v2.2 46.5% (the lab claims it leads all open-weights models under 100B total parameters on both), SWE-bench Verified 79.2%, Terminal Bench v2.1 71.6%, MCP Atlas 82.7%, BrowseComp (with compaction) 79.0%, OmniDocBench v1.6 92.8%, ARC-AGI v1/v2 84.5%/49.3%, HLE-Diamond 15.2%. [primary] (https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/)
    - Weak spots named by the lab: MultiChallenge 50.0% and ComplexConstraints v1.1 33.4%, both behind Qwen3.8-27B. Factual knowledge tracks parameter count, not looping. [primary] (https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/)
    - Model card limitations: more likely to state wrong information confidently; can get stuck in long reasoning loops, especially when debugging (the card recommends capping output tokens and agent turns); less reliable with tools and detailed instructions; "may take destructive actions without confirmation"; lower output quality outside English; looped-model support in local runtimes is new and speed varies. [primary] (https://huggingface.co/thinkingmachines/Inkling-2-Mini)
    - Hardware: about 20 GB checkpoint in mixed NVFP4/MXFP8; supported on one DGX Spark or Apple silicon with 32 GB+; Ollama on Macs with at least 32 GB; demos on a MacBook Pro M5 Pro with 48 GB. Runtimes: SGLang, vLLM, Transformers, Ollama, llama.cpp (GGUF via Unsloth), among others. [primary] (https://huggingface.co/thinkingmachines/Inkling-2-Mini; https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/)
    - Stanford intelligence-per-watt score on DGX Spark: 0.703, against 0.430 for Qwen3.6-35B-A3B. [primary] (https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/)
    - Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index: 20, second of 14 in its class. [secondary] (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W8MYFyGxSas)
    - Hands-on reviewer Sam Witteveen finds it less eager than Qwen3.8-27B to call tools and act. He relays a community suggestion to use it for planning, diagnosis and code review and hand the action-taking to a more eager model. He also reports early llama.cpp quantised builds with looping problems, and that per-pass KV caches make long-context memory behave like a 48-layer model. [secondary] (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W8MYFyGxSas)
  - **Dory1.0-4B (primary: NVIDIA paper, arXiv 2611.03998)**
    - 4B-parameter looped model on a normalised Transformer (nGPT) backbone. 72 layers: 16-layer input block, 40-layer shared recurrent block, 16-layer output block. The input block's output is re-injected before every loop; effective depth is 32 + 40k layers. 3:1 sliding-window to full attention. [primary] (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03998)
    - Pretrained on 6T tokens: about 4.5T as a normal non-looped model in NVFP4 (about 20% faster than BF16), then about 1.5T in BF16 with loop count sampled from {1,2,3,4} and truncated backprop through only the last loop; then context extended to 256K over about 300B tokens. [primary] (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03998)
    - RLVR used a "difficulty-aware loop curriculum": prompts with lower profiled pass rates are routed preferentially to deeper-loop rollout workers. In an ablation this raised Terminal-Bench v2.1 at four loops from 14.0% to 21.4%. [primary] (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03998)
    - Loop scaling (1 / 2 / 4 loops): 12-benchmark average 35.17 / 39.67 / 40.60; SWE-Bench Verified 50.3 / 59.5 / 61.8%; Terminal-Bench v2.1 12.5 / 18.1 / 22.2%; GDPval-AA v2 815 / 946 / 967 Elo; AA-Omniscience accuracy 10.03 / 10.08 / 10.03 (flat). At four loops it beats Gemma 4 26B-A4B on SWE-Bench Verified (61.8% vs 57.4%) and comes within 1.7 points of Qwen3.6-35B-A3B's average (40.60 vs 42.30). [primary] (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03998)
    - Token efficiency, k=1 to k=4: GDPval-AA v2 turns per attempt 116 → 49, output tokens per attempt 212k → 85k; Terminal-Bench 109k → 54k; SWE-Bench Verified 33.6k → 24.8k. Two loops cost 1.03–1.25× the compute of one loop yet recover 86% of the four-loop Elo gain on GDPval-AA v2 and 80% on SWE-Bench Verified; four loops cost 1.07–2.04×. The authors call k=2 "a good default". [primary] (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03998)
    - Base model: performance "tends to saturate after four loops". Evaluations at 6 and 8 loops (beyond the trained range) are roughly flat, e.g. MATH-500 69.40 at 4 and 8 loops. [primary] (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03998)
    - Last-loop KV caching keeps attention KV memory at its one-loop size for any k; at k=4 score changes range from −2.12 (AA-LCR) to +0.42 points. Self-speculative decoding (draft at k=1, verify at k=4, 4 draft tokens) gives 1.11–1.53× faster request duration with the same checkpoint. [primary] (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03998)
    - Quantisation: MXFP8 post-training quantisation stays close to BF16; NVFP4 and Q5_K_M needed quantisation-aware distillation. Up to 2.44× inference speedup over BF16 on a DGX Spark. Released under the OpenMDW-1.1 license. [primary] (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03998)
  - **Ouro (ByteDance Seed and others, arXiv 2510.25741): the research base**
    - 1.4B and 2.6B looped models trained on 7.7T tokens with 4 recurrent steps; 8 steps caused loss spikes. A learned exit gate allows early exit per token. [primary] (https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25741v2)
    - Ouro-1.4B (4 steps) vs Qwen3-4B: MATH500 82.40 vs 59.60, but lower on MMLU (67.35 vs 73.19). Gains are concentrated in math and multi-step reasoning. [primary] (https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25741v2)
    - Looping does not increase knowledge capacity: looped and non-looped models both store about 2 bits per parameter. On synthetic multi-hop QA, looped models learn the task from fewer examples. [primary] (https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25741v2)
    - KV caches: sharing caches across steps during prefill costs more than 10 points on GSM8K; at decode, keeping only the last step's cache gives GSM8K 78.85 vs 78.92 with 4× less memory. [primary] (https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25741v2)
    - RL (DAPO, GRPO) gave no significant gains over SFT, and the authors note variable-depth computation doesn't fit fast rollout engines like vLLM and SGLang. Dory and Inkling 2 Mini later did RL successfully, with fixed loop counts per rollout worker. [primary] (https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25741v2; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03998)
    - Faithfulness: probes suggest each new recurrent step can revise the provisional answer (agreement between step 2 and step 4 answers: 36.1% on 1,000 Quora question pairs). The authors stress this is observational, since they "cannot manipulate the latent reasoning process". [primary] (https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25741v2)

- Skeptic angle / open questions (for the script):
  - Almost all benchmark numbers are lab-reported. NVIDIA explicitly says its scores may differ from Artificial Analysis's.
  - Gains are uneven: knowledge and many-constraint instruction following don't improve. Inkling 2 Mini trails Qwen3.8-27B on multi-turn chat and complex constraints.
  - Extrapolation is limited. Dory saturates after about four loops; Inkling's research model is mostly trained at 3 passes and its own authors say other settings may understate what a model trained for them could do.
  - The monitorability result holds "at this scale" only. The no-CoT time horizon does rise with loops, so more capability is moving out of readable text. Thinking Machines itself says this needs tracking in larger models.
  - Local tooling is immature: different runtimes, quantisation of looped models, cache policies. "It runs on a laptop" doesn't yet mean "it runs well in your stack".
  - For Lucas specifically: the Inkling card reports lower quality outside English, so Dutch documents need their own test.

If the hardware is smaller, Dory1.0-4B at k=2 is the cheap variant to test, per NVIDIA's own default recommendation. Either way, pair it with retrieval: looping doesn't add knowledge.

- Title: Thinking in loops: Inkling 2 Mini, Dory and latent reasoning
- Summary: Looped transformers reuse the same layers several times per token. They trade chain-of-thought tokens for hidden computation, and Inkling 2 Mini and NVIDIA's Dory are the first serious open models built this way. We explain the mechanism, the cost and caching trade-offs, what it means for monitorability, and how to test whether a local looped model beats a dense one on your own tasks.
