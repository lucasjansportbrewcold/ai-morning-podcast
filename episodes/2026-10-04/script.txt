MIA: Start with the easy one.
GEORGE: Regime one. Alizadeh, Mosleh, Gilardi, Kasirzadeh and Tucker put a paper on arXiv in June called "AI Coding Agents Can Reproduce Social Science Findings." MIA: So not economics, notably.
GEORGE: Not economics, fair point. Claude Code running Opus four point six got ninety-three point four percent at the task level and seventy-eight percent at the paper level. Codex running GPT five point three got sixty-two point one and thirty-five point eight.
MIA: That's a big gap. And the paper-level number is the one I care about, because a paper only counts as reproduced if everything in it works.
GEORGE: Right, it compounds. Even if you get each table right with high probability, the chance of getting all of them right drops fast. Which is why seventy-eight versus thirty-six is a much bigger practical difference than ninety-three versus sixty-two looks.
MIA: My first reaction to a number like ninety-three percent is memorisation, though. These are published papers. Maybe the model just knows the results.
GEORGE: They checked. They anonymised the materials and asked Claude Code to identify the paper. MIA: So it mostly doesn't know what it's looking at. Not proof, but reassuring.
GEORGE: Regime two is harder and, I think, more interesting. Kohler, Zollikofer, Einsiedler, Hoyle and Ash, "Read the Paper, Write the Code." It never sees the original code, the results, or the paper itself.
MIA: Which is a much closer simulation of what a real replicator does. Or of what an author's written description is actually worth.
GEORGE: Exactly. They tested four scaffolds, Claude Code, Codex CLI, mini SWE Agent and OpenCode, crossed with four models: GPT five point four, GPT five point three Codex, Claude Opus four point six and GLM five. On forty-eight papers whose reproducibility had been verified by humans.
MIA: And the winner?
GEORGE: OpenCode with GPT five point four. Over eighty percent of its reproduced values fall inside the original ninety-five percent confidence interval, and it gets the sign right over ninety percent of the time.

MIA: Which is a real trade-off if you wanted to run this on a few hundred papers rather than forty-eight.

MIA: So sometimes the agent fails because the paper doesn't actually say what was done.
GEORGE: Right. Which turns the agent into a measuring instrument for the paper's clarity, not just for its own competence. If a strong agent can't reproduce your result from your methods section, that tells you something about your methods section.
