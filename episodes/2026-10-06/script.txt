GEORGE: The paper is from MIT, Harry Lyu and Neil Thompson. Its core idea fits in four words: right order, wrong scale. GEORGE: Exactly that kind of number. So, the framing first, because it's simple and clarifies a lot. They distinguish three kinds of validity. Ordinal: does the judge prefer the same response the human prefers? Cardinal: does it put scores at the same level, with the same spread? MIA: And the point is that the first doesn't give you the others.
GEORGE: Mathematically it can't. The ordering within a task doesn't change if you shift and stretch the whole scale. MIA: Like a thermometer that's correct about which day was warmer, but you've misread the units, so every day looks like a frost.
